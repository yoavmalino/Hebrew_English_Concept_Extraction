1 Limits

For every
$\varepsilon>0$
there is
$N\in\mathbb{N}$
with
$|a_{n}-L|<\varepsilon$
for all
$n\ge N$,
so

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}=e \tag{1}$$

A nested fraction:
$\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{x}}=\frac{x}{x+1}$
and a root
$\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$.

$$\frac{d}{dx}\sin^{2}x=2\sin x\cos x,\quad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}dx=\sqrt{\pi}$$

The binomial theorem
$(a+b)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}$
and

$$\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\le(\frac{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}}{n})^{n}$$

Vectors:
$\vec{v}\cdot\vec{w}=\|v\|\|w\|\cos\theta$
and
$f'(x)=\tilde{g}(x)$.
